# Production Pipeline Reference

## Principle

The script/narration defines meaning; the production pipeline exists to make that meaning visible. Shot count, model mode and workflow structure are derived outputs.

## Recommended sequence

1. Lock narration text and real audio timing.
2. Build a clause-level meaning ledger.
3. Freeze continuity inventory and visual-metaphor grammar.
4. Design storyboard units from semantic/visual beat boundaries.
5. Specify shots with start/event/end and transition anchors.
6. Compile model-aware prompts and job manifests.
7. Render pilot shots and review them before scale-out.
8. Render remaining units with independent retake boundaries.
9. Review every take against narration semantics.
10. Assemble only accepted takes to the locked audio.
11. Postprocess/upscale, subtitle and final technical QA.

## Continuity strategy

Continuity is not synonymous with chaining every last frame.

Use continuous handoff when the narration remains in the same conceptual/physical space and the viewer benefits from preserving spatial memory. Use a motivated cut when the narration switches example, abstraction layer, actor, metaphor or explanatory objective.

## Visual grammar for explanation

- Keep a visual system stable long enough for the viewer to learn it.
- Change one variable at a time when explaining contrast.
- Reuse a motif only when its meaning remains stable or its transformation is itself the lesson.
- Prefer visible distributions, topology and repeated structure over chart-like labels when text/UI are prohibited.
- For human agency, make the human action physically alter route/choice while AI/data systems remain tools or background infrastructure.

## Pilot policy

Before full production, test the hardest representative cases. A pilot should reveal whether the style, semantic grammar, character continuity and model prompt density are viable. Fix the grammar/workflow before spending hours on the full sequence.
